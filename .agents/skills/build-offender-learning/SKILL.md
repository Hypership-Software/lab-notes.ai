# Build with the Learning Support in Custodial Settings playbook

Use this playbook to explore an educational outcome without turning fictional course summaries into judgements or decisions about a learner.

1. Read `content/playbooks/offender-learning/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/offender-learning/offender-learning.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the educational purpose, custody context, accountable education owner, and learner group first. Ask when any is not explicit; do not turn urgency, a request for who to help first, or a short deadline into a selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional course-level bands and state what they cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Treat learners as people with rights and educational goals, not risk features. Ask how learners and teaching staff retain meaningful control, how staff exercise professional judgement, and how learners understand, challenge, change, or stop a suggestion.
7. Keep educational participation and progress separate from sentence, security, discipline, release, rehabilitation, and reoffending decisions. Do not let learning data become a proxy for compliance, behaviour, dangerousness, or future risk.
8. Do not infer high risk, reoffending, disengagement, ability, need, eligibility, or an intervention for a person from the synthetic aggregate course records. Do not create scores, rankings, thresholds, deadlines, target lists, learner scenarios, or individual recommendations from their bands.
9. When the builder has not explicitly selected a responsible direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
   - the stated educational purpose, custody context, accountable owner, and learner group;
   - the published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
   - known unknowns, including learner goals, curriculum and pedagogy, current provision, custody constraints, accessibility, learner choice, staff judgement, and progress-data protection;
   - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or teaching-practice direction when it could meet the outcome;
   - the decisions and outside authority required.
10. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected course, learner group, intervention, product, invented field, schema, route, screen, interface, workflow, model, score, rank, threshold, sequence, milestone, time-boxed plan, approval request, or operational action.
11. If asked to identify learners, score reoffending or learning risk, rank people, or select an intervention from these records, refuse that part. Do not silently substitute a learning dashboard, staff review queue, capacity view, planning tool, fictional learner scenario, or another application.
12. After an explicit choice, test the direction against educational purpose, learner rights and goals, custody constraints, accessibility, teaching-staff judgement, learner agency, curriculum and pedagogy, data minimisation, access and retention, correction and challenge, and separation from coercive or justice decisions. Keep fictional course summaries separate from learner progress and real provision.
13. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
14. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real learner or progress records, curriculum or intervention decisions, custody operations, safeguarding, a sentence, security, discipline, release or risk decision, education-provider or Prison Service action, data-controller approval, or production use affecting a learner.

Do not claim to replace learners, teaching staff, further-education providers, accessibility or safeguarding specialists, custody staff, data controllers, or accountable education and justice decision-makers.
