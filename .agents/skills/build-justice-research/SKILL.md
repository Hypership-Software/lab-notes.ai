# Build with the Justice Research and Analysis playbook

Use this playbook to explore a descriptive research outcome without turning fictional justice-system activity into a risk or enforcement decision.

1. Read `content/playbooks/justice-research/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/justice-research/justice-research.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the intended research outcome, unit of analysis, accountable owner, prohibited downstream uses, and affected communities first. Ask when any is not explicit; do not treat urgency, a request for a target, or a short deadline as a selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional aggregate rows and state what they cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Keep descriptive research separate from prediction, enforcement, sentencing, legal advice, and individual or area risk assessment. A recorded disposal or processing time is not evidence of crime prevalence, harm, culpability, need for enforcement, future behaviour, or who should be acted on.
7. Require traceability for every research claim: identify the exact authorised source material, definitions, data cells or records, transformation, limitations, and responsible review. A publication link or synthetic record ID alone does not make an interpretation traceable or valid.
8. Test representativeness before interpretation. Recorded activity reflects who enters and moves through institutional processes; the registered sources and fictional rows do not establish who or what is missing. Involve affected communities early enough to change or stop the question, method, terminology, permitted use, or publication—not to endorse a finished answer.
9. Do not rank or target people, communities, areas, offence groups, or records; infer individual risk; recommend enforcement, sentencing, or another coercive action; or design a predictive model from the synthetic data. Do not select the longest fictional processing time as a priority.
10. When the builder has not explicitly selected a responsible direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
   - the stated research outcome, unit of analysis, accountable owner, prohibited uses, and affected communities;
   - the published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
   - known unknowns, including definitions, source cycles, population and missingness, representativeness, traceability, institutional effects, affected perspectives, and decision authority;
   - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or participatory research direction when it could meet the outcome;
   - the decisions and outside authority required.
11. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected question, target or offence group, product, invented field, schema, route, screen, interface, workflow, model, score, rank, threshold, sequence, milestone, time-boxed plan, approval request, or operational action.
12. If asked for a person or area risk score, target, ranking, enforcement recommendation, sentence recommendation, or predictive model, refuse that part. Do not silently substitute a justice research workspace, evidence explorer, traceability ledger, source register, descriptive dashboard, or another application.
13. After an explicit choice, test the direction against research purpose, source definitions and cycles, population and denominator, missingness, representativeness, institutional effects, reproducible traceability, alternative explanations, affected-community challenge, privacy, disclosure, downstream use, and accountable authority. Keep description separate from causal or operational interpretation.
14. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
15. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires person-level, case-level, linked, protected, restricted, or small-area data; legal interpretation; publication of official findings; or an enforcement, prosecution, bail, sentencing, release, supervision, eligibility, or other coercive decision.

Do not claim to replace people and communities affected by justice processes, victims and witnesses, justice researchers and statisticians, legal or human-rights specialists, practitioners, data controllers, or accountable public-service and judicial decision-makers.
