# Justice Research and Analysis — domain brief

## Domain vocabulary

- **Descriptive research:** work that states what authorised data records for a defined unit, population, place, and period. It does not by itself explain causes, predict outcomes, or authorise action.
- **Recorded justice-system activity:** prosecutions, convictions, disposals, processing times, or other events captured by institutional processes. The playbook warns that recorded patterns reflect reporting, stopping, and prosecution as well as behaviour.
- **Representativeness:** whether evidence covers the people, events, places, periods, and perspectives needed for the stated question. The registered aggregate sources and synthetic file do not establish this for a future analysis.
- **Traceability:** an inspectable path from a claim to exact authorised source material, definitions, input cells or records, transformations, limitations, and responsible review. A source link alone establishes location, not this complete path.
- **Exploratory pattern:** a difference or association observed in data. The playbook states that it is not a cause and must never become a reason to act on an individual.
- **Individual or area risk assessment:** a claim about a person, group, community, or place that could guide scrutiny or action. The committed file contains no person, case, location, or valid risk outcome.
- **Downstream coercive use:** use in enforcement, investigation, prosecution, bail, sentencing, detention, release, supervision, eligibility, or another decision that can constrain a person or community. Descriptive research must remain separate from it.

## Organisations and people affected

The Department of Justice publishes both registered sources. One is a yearly bulletin on court prosecutions, convictions, and out-of-court disposals by court type, offence category, and disposal. The other is a collection spanning separate justice statistics and research publications on different subjects and cycles. These sources do not assign ownership of a future question or authorise predictive, enforcement, sentencing, or individual-risk use.

People and communities affected by justice processes, victims and witnesses, people accused or prosecuted, communities exposed to enforcement, justice researchers and statisticians, practitioners, legal and human-rights specialists, data controllers, and accountable public-service and judicial roles could all be affected by future work. This is a project stakeholder map, not evidence that any role has agreed to participate. Affected communities must be able to change or stop a research question and its permitted uses early, not merely review a completed output.

## Source map and access

- **Department of Justice — [Court Prosecutions, Convictions and Out of Court Disposals Statistics for Northern Ireland](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/publications/court-prosecutions-convictions-and-out-court-disposals-statistics-northern-ireland-2025):** open yearly statistics broken down by court type, offence category, and disposal. This is the published aggregate shape the fictional records imitate. It contains no case or person identifier and does not measure who or where needs enforcement.
- **Department of Justice — [Statistics and Research](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/topics/statistics-and-research-justice):** an open collection of separate publications covering courts and tribunals, prosecutions, case processing, prisons, reoffending, the Safe Community Survey, youth justice, and research with victims and witnesses. Its subjects, definitions, and publication cycles are not automatically compatible.

Treat both as **Real published source** material. Treat the strategy proposal, playbook summary, purpose, preparation, limitations, and caveats as project interpretation. Treat the committed JSON as **Synthetic working data**, never as official court data, a real case, a prevalence measure, representative evidence, training data, a priority, or authority for action.

## Synthetic fields

- `id` is a fictional record identifier.
- `offenceGroup` is one of five broad project-authored labels. It is not a person, case, location, risk class, enforcement category, or complete justice taxonomy.
- `quarter` is one of four fictional 2025 quarters. It is not an official reporting period.
- `disposalsBand` is an invented aggregate range. It is not an exact count, population denominator, prevalence measure, or estimate of harm.
- `medianDaysToDisposal` is an invented duration. The file does not supply the start and end definitions, included cases, missing cases, process changes, distribution, or causes.

The 20 rows contain no people, cases, allegations, outcomes at person level, places, population or exposure denominators, reporting access, enforcement activity, victimisation measure, protected characteristic, lawful purpose, or risk outcome. Do not convert bands to invented exact values, rank records or offence groups, call longer processing a target, infer cause, or attach any enforcement or sentencing action.

## Known unknowns

- The research outcome, unit of analysis, accountable owner, prohibited downstream uses, and affected communities are unknown until stated.
- Exact published table definitions, coverage, exclusions, revisions, classifications, and processing-time start and end points have not been established from the registered descriptions.
- The two registered sources lead to publications on different subjects and cycles; compatibility is not established.
- Population and exposure denominators, people and events absent from recorded processes, missing or suppressed cells, linkage error, and changes in institutional practice are not represented.
- Whether recorded activity represents the population or phenomenon relevant to a future question is unknown.
- Source URLs exist, but claim-level links to exact authorised cells, definitions, transformations, limitations, and review do not.
- The effects of reporting, stopping, investigation, prosecution, and disposal choices on recorded patterns are unresolved.
- Affected-community perspectives, foreseeable downstream uses, challenge routes, correction, disclosure, and publication authority are not established.
- The registered sources do not establish that a justice research workspace, evidence explorer, traceability ledger, dashboard, software product, or AI is required.

## Assumptions to challenge

- A higher fictional disposal band does not establish prevalence, harm, workload, importance, risk, or need for enforcement.
- A longer fictional median time does not identify a person, area, offence group, process, or service to target and does not explain its cause.
- Four fictional quarters cannot establish a trend, process change, causal effect, or future outcome.
- Recorded activity cannot be treated as underlying behaviour without examining institutional and access effects.
- A source link or record ID does not make a claim traceable when definitions, transformations, limitations, or review are missing.
- Transparent calculations, human review, or a descriptive label cannot make an unsupported inference representative or suitable for coercive use.
- Inviting affected communities to approve a finished tool is not the same as giving them power to shape or stop the question and its uses.
- Manual source review, definition alignment, participatory research framing, or deciding not to build may meet the outcome without a predictive model or software product.

## Questions before building

- What descriptive research outcome is intended, for which unit and population, who owns it, and which decisions must it never influence?
- Which exact publication, table, definition, period, classification, quality note, and revision supports each proposed claim?
- Who and what enters the recorded process, who or what is absent, and is there a valid denominator for the question?
- How might reporting access and institutional choices about stopping, investigation, prosecution, and disposal shape the observed pattern?
- How will each claim remain traceable to authorised source material, input cells or records, transformations, limitations, and responsible review?
- Which alternative explanations, missing data, incompatible definitions, suppressed values, linkage errors, and process changes must remain visible?
- Which affected communities must shape the question, terminology, method, permitted uses, and publication early enough to change or stop the work?
- Could manual source review, definition work, participatory framing, statistical-owner inquiry, or another non-tool direction meet the outcome?
- Where must repository work stop because data access, statistical ownership, legal interpretation, community authority, or a coercive decision is outside scope?

## Safety and service constraints

Keep all 20 rows visibly labelled **Synthetic working data** and separate from the two real published sources. Use them only to inspect a safe aggregate file shape; do not derive a target, priority, risk, enforcement need, sentencing view, legal conclusion, predictive model, or operational action. Preserve bands as bands and uncertainty as uncertainty.

Require claim-level traceability and test representativeness before interpretation. Keep affected-community challenge early and consequential. Separate description from causation, prediction, enforcement, sentencing, and individual or area risk assessment. Do not prescribe a research workspace, evidence explorer, traceability ledger, source register, dashboard, or other substitute before the builder chooses a responsible direction. Stop wherever person-level, case-level, restricted, protected, or small-area data, legal interpretation, official publication, or coercive authority is required.
