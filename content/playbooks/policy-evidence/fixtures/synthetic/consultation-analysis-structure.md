# How published consultation analysis is structured

This note is written by this project. It records the *shape* of published
consultation analysis so that the synthetic corpus in this directory has a
realistic structure instead of an invented one.

It was informed by reading the `circular-economy-consultation-report` source
recorded in this playbook's source register. It reproduces no text from that
report or from any other source. Nothing here is a quotation, an extract, or a
sample of official material, and no respondent is represented.

## What a published response report tends to contain

- Background to the consultation and the proposal it covered.
- How the consultation was run: the channels used, the period it was open, and
  the questions asked.
- Who responded, described only in aggregate categories such as individuals,
  community organisations, businesses, and public bodies.
- A summary of views, organised question by question or theme by theme.
- The department's response to each theme, kept visibly separate from the
  summary of what respondents said.
- Next steps, and annexes listing the consultation questions and the
  organisations that agreed to be named.

## The analytical stages between response and publication

1. Responses are received and logged.
2. Personal information is removed or redacted before analysis begins.
3. Answers are separated by consultation question.
4. Each answer is read and given one or more initial labels.
5. Labels are grouped into themes, and related themes into sub-themes.
6. Disagreement and minority views are deliberately retained rather than
   averaged away.
7. Coverage is counted by how many responses raised a theme, not by how many
   times it was mentioned.
8. Illustrative material is quoted only where permission allows.
9. Theme summaries are drafted, then reviewed by the responsible policy leads.
10. The analysis is published alongside the department's own response.

## Vocabulary the analysis uses

Respondent, response, consultation question, theme, sub-theme, minority view,
representation, call for evidence, aggregate, verbatim, redaction.

## What the synthetic corpus takes from this note

The corpus borrows the themes, the four broad positions a response can take,
and this register of plain public-service English. It borrows no content. Every
document in `corpus.json` is invented, is labelled `Synthetic working data`, and
describes no real consultation, organisation, place, or person.

The six themes and the four positions are not observed in the source report;
they are chosen by this project, for this exemplar:

- **Themes:** access to services, workforce capability, data governance,
  accountability, procurement and reuse, and environmental cost.
- **Positions:** supportive, critical, mixed, and uncertain.
