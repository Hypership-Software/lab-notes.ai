# Build with the Violence Risk Pattern Research playbook

Use this playbook to decide whether there is a responsible direction to explore without turning fictional people or linked histories into risk evidence.

1. Read `content/playbooks/violence-risk-research/playbook.ts`.
2. Confirm that `syntheticData.status` is `not-responsible`. There is no dataset to load: treat its absence as a deliberate safety boundary, not an asset gap to fill.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the builder's intended outcome, affected people, accountable owner, and permitted decision first. If the request states neither a usable exploration outcome nor a usable purpose, ask a clarifying question before the brief. If a usable exploration outcome or purpose is stated but affected people, the accountable owner, or the permitted decision is missing, record each missing item as a known unknown and still return the neutral decision brief with multiple unranked approaches. Do not turn urgency, a delivery deadline, or a request for something tangible into a selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** state that this playbook deliberately contains no synthetic working data.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Keep descriptive research, prevention and support-service questions, person-level inference, and operational decisions separate. Aggregate counts or survey findings cannot establish individual behaviour, need, safety, eligibility, or risk.
7. Do not invent or request person-shaped records, case histories, linked timelines, personas, or individual risk scores. Stop before operational prediction, targeting, monitoring, investigation, enforcement, or a decision about any person.
8. If asked for fictional people, scores, a target list, or an operational prediction, refuse that part. Do not silently substitute a research-readiness application, evidence ledger, governance dashboard, service map, fictional personas, case timelines, or another product.
9. When the builder has not explicitly selected a responsible direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
   - the stated outcome, affected people, accountable owner, and permitted decision;
   - the published evidence, project interpretation, and no-dataset boundary;
   - known unknowns, including service or prevention capacity, lived-experience ownership, lawful purpose, research governance, secure access, bias, safeguarding, redress, and evaluation authority;
   - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or service-design direction when it could meet the outcome;
   - the decisions and outside authority required.
10. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected product, substitute application, invented field, schema, route, screen, interface, workflow, model, score, threshold, sequence, milestone, time-boxed plan, approval request, or operational action.
11. After an explicit choice, test the direction against service purpose, affected-community and lived-experience ownership, source representativeness, reporting and institutional-contact bias, false-positive and false-negative harms, safe support capacity, lawful access, data protection, ethics, safeguarding, security, redress, evaluation, and accountable authority. Keep public aggregate evidence separate from any future governed person-level research.
12. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
13. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires person-level or linked records, secure research access, ethics or data-protection approval, legal interpretation, safeguarding decisions, service-owner action, contact with a person, operational prediction, targeting, enforcement, or another coercive decision.

Do not claim to replace people affected by violence, people with lived experience, specialist community and voluntary organisations, support and prevention services, safeguarding professionals, researchers, ethics and governance bodies, legal advisers, data controllers, or accountable justice, health, and public-service decision-makers.
