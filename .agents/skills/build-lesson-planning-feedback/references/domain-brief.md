# Lesson Planning and Feedback Support — domain brief

## Domain vocabulary

- **Lesson planning:** use this project term for a teacher's work in shaping intended learning, content, activities, checks, resources, and adaptations for a particular context. The registered sources do not describe how an individual teacher plans.
- **Feedback support:** the strategy example names consistent feedback as a possible application. The dataset contains no pupil work, teacher feedback, response, revision, or outcome, so feedback cannot be evaluated from it.
- **Curriculum reference:** CCEA's [Curriculum at Key Stage 3](https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-3/curriculum) sets out areas of learning, subject strands, and skills for Key Stage 3. A link or matching subject name is a reference to inspect, not proof that a draft is correct or compliant.
- **Teacher authorship:** use this project term for the teacher retaining ownership of the purpose, words, edits, choices, rejected suggestions, and final material.
- **Professional judgement:** use this project term for decisions that require a teacher's knowledge of subject, curriculum, pupils, context, and consequences. Polished text cannot replace that judgement.
- **Feedback provenance:** use this project term for making the origin and changes of source work, a suggestion, an edit, an approval, and final feedback inspectable. The registered sources do not define a reusable provenance model.
- **Teacher workload:** the strategy proposal says support could save time, but the registered sources provide no measure of planning or feedback workload and no evidence that a tool would reduce it.

## Organisations and people affected

CCEA publishes the Key Stage 3 curriculum reference. The Department of Education publishes school-enrolment figures. Teachers, pupils, curriculum and subject specialists, accessibility specialists, safeguarding leads, school leaders, guardians, and data controllers could be affected by future lesson-planning or feedback work, but the registered sources do not assign their responsibilities or authorise an application.

The synthetic records contain no real teacher or pupil material. The sources do not describe classroom context, planning practice, teacher workload, subject accuracy, accessibility, additional needs, home languages, feedback practice, pupil response, or outcomes. Do not infer these from a fictional objective or an enrolment count.

## Source map and access

- **Council for the Curriculum, Examinations and Assessment — [Curriculum at Key Stage 3](https://ccea.org.uk/key-stage-3/curriculum):** open curriculum material setting out Key Stage 3 areas of learning, subject strands, and skills. It is relevant to the Key Stage 3 subject labels in the dataset. It does not validate a fictional objective, a lesson draft, a pedagogical choice, or material labelled Key Stage 2 or Key Stage 4.
- **Department of Education — [School enrolments](https://www.education-ni.gov.uk/topics/school-enrolments):** open enrolment figures, including school-level data and population projections. It provides scale context and no evidence about an individual teacher's planning, workload, support need, or feedback.

Treat both as **Real published source** material. Treat the playbook summary, caveats, and dataset preparation as project interpretation. Treat the committed JSON as **Synthetic working data**, never as teacher-authored planning, pupil evidence, curriculum assurance, or proof of time saved.

## Synthetic fields

- `id` is a fictional record key.
- `keyStage` is a project-authored context label. The registered curriculum source covers Key Stage 3 only, so the field does not verify Key Stage 2 or Key Stage 4 content.
- `subject` is a project-authored subject or area label and does not establish curriculum alignment or subject accuracy.
- `topic` is a fictional planning label, not a reviewed curriculum unit or priority.
- `draftObjective` is an AI-authored rough lesson objective, not a teacher's work and not evidence of teaching quality, workload, pupil need, or curriculum correctness.

The 18 records contain no teacher, pupil, school, class, lesson plan, pupil work, feedback, assessment, accessibility need, safeguarding information, outcome, revision, or time measure. Do not select or rank records as a proxy for support need, create fictional pupil feedback, or infer anything about a person from their wording.

## Known unknowns

- The intended teaching outcome, educational setting, age range, subject context, and accountable owner are unknown until stated.
- Curriculum coverage beyond the registered Key Stage 3 reference is not supplied.
- Subject accuracy, curriculum alignment, progression, pedagogy, and suitable evidence of learning have not been reviewed.
- No teacher planning practice, workload baseline, time measure, or account of where administrative burden occurs is registered.
- How a teacher would preserve authorship, understand provenance, reject suggestions, correct errors, and record their judgement is not established.
- The accessibility and inclusion needs of a class, including language, device, sensory, cognitive, physical, and supported routes, are not present.
- No pupil work, feedback purpose, success criteria, provenance, teacher approval, pupil response, correction route, or learning outcome is available.
- The purpose, lawful basis, minimisation, access, retention, correction, safeguarding, and data-controller decisions for real pupil work are unknown.
- No measure establishes whether consistent wording improves feedback, reduces workload, changes learning, or creates exclusion.
- Responsibility for subject review, curriculum assurance, safeguarding, final teaching material, and feedback remains to be established for a future direction.

## Assumptions to challenge

- A rough fictional objective does not identify a teacher who needs support or a high-priority lesson.
- Ranking fictional objectives does not measure teacher workload, teaching quality, urgency, or pupil need.
- A polished draft can still be wrong about subject, age, curriculum, pedagogy, or context.
- A curriculum link or matching label does not make material curriculum compliant.
- Consistent feedback wording does not by itself make feedback accurate, useful, inclusive, or fair.
- Teacher approval does not repair missing provenance or make unsupported subject content correct.
- Standard wording may exclude pupils with additional needs, other home languages, or different circumstances, as the playbook caveat states.
- The shape of synthetic planning notes does not represent how teachers plan in any school.
- The registered sources do not establish that a drafting assistant, queue, feedback product, software, or AI is required.

## Questions before building

- What teaching outcome is intended, in which educational context, and who is accountable for it?
- Which curriculum and subject authority applies, and what published material must be checked?
- What part of planning or feedback work creates burden, how is that known, and what must remain teacher-authored?
- How will the teacher inspect sources, distinguish suggestions, edit or reject material, and retain final professional responsibility?
- What would demonstrate subject accuracy, curriculum fit, appropriate progression, and pedagogical usefulness without treating polish as correctness?
- Whose work or feedback is being used, who authored each part, what changed, who approved it, and how can an error be corrected?
- Which accessible formats, languages, devices, adaptations, assisted routes, and non-digital alternatives must be supported?
- If real pupil work is proposed, what purpose, authority, minimisation, access, retention, correction, safeguarding, and data-controller decisions are required?
- Could teacher collaboration, curriculum guidance, shared planning practice, workload change, templates, or another non-tool direction meet the outcome?
- Where must work stop because subject, curriculum, safeguarding, school, or data authority is unavailable?

## Safety and service constraints

Preserve teacher authorship and professional judgement from the first decision. Keep source material, project interpretation, synthetic notes, suggestions, teacher edits, approvals, and final material visibly distinct. Do not rank teachers or fictional objectives as proxies for support need, and never treat fluent or polished wording as curriculum correctness.

The committed dataset contains neither pupil work nor feedback. Do not invent them or infer pupil ability, need, attainment, behaviour, or support from a fictional record. Stop before using real teacher planning or pupil work until the responsible school, safeguarding lead, and data controller establish purpose and authority. Curriculum and subject claims require accountable professional review; the repository's source link and synthetic notes cannot provide assurance.
