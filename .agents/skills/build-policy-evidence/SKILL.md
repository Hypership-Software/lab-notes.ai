# Build with the Policy Evidence playbook

1. Read `content/playbooks/policy-evidence/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/policy-evidence/policy-evidence.data.json`.
3. Read `content/playbooks/policy-evidence/consultation-analysis-structure.md`; treat it as project interpretation, not a published source.
4. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
5. Establish the outcome and consultation purpose before proposing an application, ranking, data model, route, or implementation. Ask the builder when the outcome is not explicit.
6. Label each claim as one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify analysis or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional working data and state what it cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** state the assumption and what evidence or authority would be needed.
7. Treat a theme as a transparent organising aid, not a finding, policy conclusion, priority, prevalence claim, or recommendation. Never rank policy themes from the synthetic corpus.
8. Link every summary or classification to its exact authorised passage. When a passage is unavailable, redacted, or untraceable, state that limit and do not call the claim traceable.
9. Preserve disagreement, minority views, uncertainty, corpus-completeness limits, and potentially missed language. Keep analysis of responses separate from the accountable policy response or decision.
10. Do not treat the deleted workbench, its theme board, ranking, fields, routes, workflow, or implementation journey as a preferred or default solution.
11. When the builder has not explicitly selected a direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
    - the stated outcome and consultation purpose;
    - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
    - known unknowns, including corpus completeness, theme definitions, passage traceability, disagreement, uncertainty, missed language, and decision ownership;
    - at least two parallel credible approaches with trade-offs, kept unranked and including a manual, non-tool, or service-design approach when it could meet the outcome;
    - the decisions and outside authority required.
12. End that brief by asking the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Give no ranking, recommended application, chosen first build, invented data model, route, workflow, implementation steps, milestone plan, time-boxed build, or approval request before that choice. Urgency or a request to choose what to build first does not replace the builder's selection.
13. After an explicit choice, test the direction against consultation purpose, corpus completeness, theme contestability, passage traceability, disagreement, uncertainty, missed language, confidentiality, and accountable policy ownership. Treat unresolved items as unknown.
14. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
15. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real responses, person-level or restricted data, legal interpretation, publication of analysis, an official policy response, or decision-maker approval.

Do not claim to replace respondents, consultation analysts, subject-matter specialists, policy teams, service owners, legal advisers, data controllers, or accountable decision-makers.
