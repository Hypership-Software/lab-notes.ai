# Build with the Traffic Flow Management playbook

Use this playbook to explore a transport outcome without turning fictional counts into a junction choice, control policy, or claim about who benefits.

1. Read `content/playbooks/traffic-flow/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/traffic-flow/traffic-flow.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the intended outcome, spatial and temporal scope, accountable owner, and people affected first. Ask when any is not explicit; do not treat urgency, a request for the best junction, or a delivery deadline as a safe selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional working data and state what it cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Keep these concepts separate:
   - **Monitoring** observes a defined measure at a stated place and time; a count does not establish congestion, delay, capacity, or cause.
   - **Modelling** estimates an outcome under stated assumptions; it does not prove what a signal change would do.
   - **Control** changes infrastructure or an approved operating plan and requires accountable transport and safety authority.
   - **Displacement** is a change in traffic, delay, risk, or burden beyond the measured location or group; four unconnected fictional junctions cannot reveal it.
   - **Public-realm outcomes** concern how a place works for people beyond vehicle throughput and are not measured by this dataset.
7. Ask who is affected by any proposed optimisation, whose outcomes are absent, who may be disadvantaged by displacement, and who has authority to define the objective and trade-offs. Vehicle counts and vehicle shares do not represent pedestrians, journey purpose, passenger numbers, accessibility needs, adjacent streets, or public-realm experience.
8. Do not rank or select a synthetic junction or time band, invent objective weights or targets, choose a control strategy, propose signal timings or plans, forecast demand, simulate effects, recommend a change, or describe a rollback threshold from these records.
9. If asked for junction selection, optimisation weights, performance targets, a control strategy, or signal changes from this dataset, refuse that part. Do not silently substitute a monitoring dashboard, demand model, recommendation tool, scenario lab, shadow controller, or another application.
10. When the builder has not explicitly selected a responsible direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
    - the stated outcome and scope;
    - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
    - known unknowns, including network context, measurement coverage, modelling ground truth, control authority, displacement, public-realm outcomes, and affected stakeholders;
    - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or service-design direction when it could meet the outcome;
    - the decisions and outside authority required.
11. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, junction selection, ranking, target, weight, threshold, signal or control strategy, invented field, schema, route, screen, interface, workflow, model, sequence, milestone, time-boxed plan, operational action, approval request, or substitute application.
12. After an explicit choice, test the direction against spatial and temporal coverage, missing modes and people, data quality, network effects, displacement, public-realm outcomes, accessibility and equity, safety, operator and infrastructure authority, and the distinction between monitoring, modelling, and control. Keep fictional counts separate from published facts and authorised operational evidence.
13. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
14. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires a real junction or network, detector or controller data, signal plans, safety timings, congestion or delay interpretation, optimisation objectives, live or shadow control, public-realm decisions, legal interpretation, data-controller approval, or transport-authority action.

Do not claim to replace people travelling through or living near an affected place, transport-control operators, signal engineers, road-safety professionals, accessibility or equality specialists, public-realm decision-makers, legal advisers, data controllers, or accountable transport authorities.
