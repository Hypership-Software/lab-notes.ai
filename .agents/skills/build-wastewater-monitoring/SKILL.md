# Build with the Wastewater Monitoring playbook

Use this playbook to explore a monitoring outcome without turning fictional sparse readings into a health, compliance, or operational conclusion.

1. Read `content/playbooks/wastewater-monitoring/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/wastewater-monitoring/wastewater-monitoring.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the intended outcome and accountable owner first. Ask when either is not explicit; do not treat urgency, a request for hotspots or alerts, or a delivery deadline as a safe selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional working data and state what it cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Distinguish these activities and do not collapse them:
   - **Monitoring** observes a defined signal over a stated place and time.
   - **Investigation** tests possible explanations using authorised context and professional judgement.
   - **Regulatory action** depends on applicable standards, procedures, and accountable authority outside this repository.
7. Preserve spatial and temporal uncertainty. The six works are fictional letters without locations, receiving waters, or network relationships, and three weekly readings per works do not establish a baseline, trend, seasonality, persistence, or geographical hotspot.
8. Do not infer a health risk, environmental hotspot, incident, consent breach, compliance state, safe state, or required action from the synthetic readings. Do not invent or apply an alert threshold, anomaly rule, site ranking, risk score, or regulatory limit.
9. If asked for hotspot claims, health-risk claims, alerts, compliance conclusions, or automatic action from this dataset, refuse that part. Do not silently substitute an internal triage queue, anomaly detector, site index, map, notification, investigation workflow, or another application.
10. When the builder has not explicitly selected a responsible direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
    - the stated outcome;
    - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
    - known unknowns, including signal definition, sampling and quality context, spatial and temporal coverage, ground truth, operational authority, and affected stakeholders;
    - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or service-design direction when it could meet the outcome;
    - the decisions and outside authority required.
11. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected product, invented threshold, field, schema, route, screen, interface, workflow, model, sequence, milestone, time-boxed plan, alert, action, approval request, or substitute application.
12. After an explicit choice, test the direction against sampling validity, source coverage, spatial and temporal limits, missing ground truth, environmental interpretation, network-detail sensitivity, affected groups, and operational ownership. Keep fictional readings separate from real published sources and unsupported assumptions.
13. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
14. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real operational or network data, environmental or health interpretation, an alert threshold, incident declaration, compliance judgement, investigation decision, public communication, regulatory action, legal interpretation, data-controller approval, or operator action.

Do not claim to replace wastewater operators, environmental investigators, regulators, public-health professionals, laboratories, network or security owners, legal advisers, data controllers, or accountable service owners.
