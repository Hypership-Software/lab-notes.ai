# Build with the life-event-services playbook

1. Read `content/playbooks/life-event-services/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/life-event-services/life-event-services.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Ask what outcome the builder wants to explore before proposing implementation.
5. Separate published facts, project interpretation, synthetic values, and unsupported assumptions in every recommendation.
6. When the builder has not explicitly selected an approach, return only a neutral decision brief in this order: evidence, known unknowns, at least two parallel credible approaches, their trade-offs, and the decisions and authority required. Include a non-AI or service-design approach when it could meet the outcome. Keep the approaches unranked and give none recommended, better, best, or chosen status.
7. End the decision brief by asking the builder to choose a direction. Wait for that choice before giving implementation steps, routes, states, milestones, or a time-boxed build definition; urgency or a request for something tangible does not replace the choice.
8. If a notification request lacks authority, name what cannot proceed and return to the neutral decision brief without substituting another product.
9. Treat authority, consent, matching errors, accessibility, distress, and fraud exposure as design inputs rather than end-of-plan disclaimers.
10. Do not select a recipient, field set, or solution without authority in a published source or from the responsible source owner.
11. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the typed playbook contract, privacy rules, and repository quality gates before changing code.
12. Stop and name the missing authority when the work requires real bereavement records, production notifications, legal interpretation, or service-owner approval.

Do not claim to replace service owners, legal advisers, registrars, bereavement professionals, affected families, or data controllers.
