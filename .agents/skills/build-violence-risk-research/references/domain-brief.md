# Violence Risk Pattern Research — domain brief

## Domain vocabulary

- **Descriptive research:** published counts, surveys, and research that describe recorded patterns at their stated population, place, and period. Descriptive evidence does not predict an individual's behaviour, safety, need, or future.
- **Person-shaped record:** use this project term for an invented or real record whose fields describe one person, household, case, linked history, or timeline. A useful stand-in for the strategy example would have to be person-shaped because its proposed output concerns who is most at risk.
- **Individual risk score:** a value or category that claims to estimate a person's likelihood of violence, victimisation, or another outcome. The registered sources provide no person-level input, validated outcome, or authority for one.
- **Reporting pattern:** what institutions record and publish. The playbook warns that this is not individual behaviour and can reflect who comes into contact with institutions.
- **Prevention or support-service question:** a question about services, capacity, accessibility, or prevention that must be owned by the responsible organisations and affected people. It is not an automatic substitute for the strategy example.
- **Governed research access:** use this project term for formal access under ethics and data-protection approval, with the research question owned from the start by domain specialists and people with lived experience. The playbook states this as a prerequisite, not a route this repository can authorise.

## Organisations and people affected

The Executive Office publishes the Ending Violence Against Women and Girls material and describes a programme involving government, community and voluntary organisations, and commissioned research. The Department of Justice publishes justice statistics, surveys, and research. These public sources do not assign ownership of a future research question, establish a linked-records project, or authorise action about an individual.

People affected by violence, people with lived experience, specialist community and voluntary organisations, prevention and support services, justice and health organisations, researchers, safeguarding professionals, legal and data-protection roles, and accountable public-service owners could all be affected by future work. This is a project stakeholder map, not evidence that any person or organisation has agreed to participate. People with lived experience and domain specialists must own a person-level research question from the start rather than review a finished answer.

## Source map and access

- **The Executive Office — [Ending Violence Against Women and Girls](https://www.executiveoffice-ni.gov.uk/topics/ending-violence-against-women-and-girls):** open material covering the Northern Ireland strategic framework, delivery plans, commissioned research, and funding routed to community and voluntary organisations. The playbook interprets this as a service- and prevention-led context; it does not publish linked person-level records or validate risk prediction.
- **Department of Justice — [Statistics and Research](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/topics/statistics-and-research-justice):** open statistics, surveys, and research including reoffending bulletins, the Safe Community Survey, and work with victims and witnesses. The playbook records that this material consists of counts and survey findings, far from the linked person-level records assumed by the strategy example.

Treat both as **Real published source** material. Treat the strategy proposal, summary, responsible refusal, caveats, and stated prerequisites as project interpretation. Neither source establishes that a model, score, target list, research-readiness product, or other software is necessary or authorised.

## Synthetic fields

There are no synthetic fields and no dataset for this playbook. That absence is deliberate. A stand-in capable of supporting the proposed person-level task would need to describe a person's history, identity-linked events, or outcomes; it would therefore become person-shaped even if names were removed or every row were fictional. Safe aggregate bands would no longer represent the individual-level task and could not validate a score or targeting decision.

Do not create invented identifiers, demographics, justice or health histories, relationships, reports, incidents, service contacts, locations, timestamps, labels, scores, ranks, flags, referrals, or outcomes. Do not disguise person-shaped information as personas, cases, cohorts of one, linked timelines, or synthetic fixtures.

## Known unknowns

- The intended outcome, affected people, accountable owner, and permitted decision are unknown until stated.
- The registered sources do not establish that person-level prediction is needed, effective, lawful, safe, or better than service and prevention work.
- No linked justice and health records, lawful purpose, access route, data controller, secure research environment, ethics approval, or data-protection approval is available in the repository.
- No individual outcome definition, observation window, sampling frame, linkage method, missingness account, reference standard, or independent validation evidence exists.
- The representativeness of recorded activity and the effects of reporting and institutional-contact patterns are unknown.
- The harms of false positives and false negatives, disclosure, unsafe contact, stigma, discrimination, surveillance, and withdrawal or denial of help have not been resolved.
- The availability, capacity, accessibility, safety, consent, and accountability of any prevention or support response are not established.
- The registered sources do not establish that a research-readiness application, evidence ledger, governance dashboard, service map, software product, or AI is required.

## Assumptions to challenge

- Published counts and surveys cannot establish who is at risk or why an individual event occurred.
- A fictional person can still encode unsupported and harmful assumptions about danger, victimisation, vulnerability, or institutional contact.
- Removing names or calling records synthetic does not make person-shaped histories suitable evidence.
- Reporting and institutional-contact patterns are not neutral measures of behaviour or need.
- A transparent score, human review, or governance screen cannot supply missing validity, lawful purpose, safe services, or accountable authority.
- Identifying someone is not beneficial when no safe, accessible, and authorised response exists.
- A research-readiness product is still a selected application; refusing prediction does not justify prescribing that substitute.
- Manual research, service and prevention inquiry, participatory problem framing, or deciding not to build may meet the outcome without person-level data or AI.

## Questions before building

- What outcome is intended, for whom, who owns it, and what decision—if any—is permitted to follow?
- Is the question descriptive research, service or prevention inquiry, person-level inference, or an operational decision?
- What do the registered aggregate sources establish, and which claims remain project interpretation or unsupported assumptions?
- Which people with lived experience, specialist organisations, support and prevention services, and affected communities must own the question?
- What safe help would exist, who can provide it, how would a person exercise choice, and what happens if capacity is absent?
- Could manual research, participatory service design, prevention work, or another non-tool direction meet the outcome?
- If a future research question genuinely requires person-level records, who can establish lawful purpose, ethics and data-protection approval, secure access, safeguarding, redress, evaluation, and accountable ownership?
- How would reporting and institutional-contact bias, missingness, linkage error, false positives, false negatives, disclosure, unsafe contact, and downstream coercive use be examined?
- Where must repository work stop because evidence, service capacity, professional judgement, governed access, or public authority is unavailable?

## Safety and service constraints

Keep this repository at public aggregate evidence and responsible question-framing level. Treat the absent synthetic dataset as a deliberate boundary. Do not request, generate, commit, or process person-shaped records, linked histories, case timelines, personas, individual scores, ranks, target lists, alerts, or operational recommendations.

Do not predict, identify, prioritise, monitor, contact, investigate, sanction, or enforce against a person. Do not turn a responsible refusal into a research-readiness application, evidence ledger, governance dashboard, service map, or clinical or justice-looking demonstration. If a selected direction requires person-level or linked records, stop for formal governed research access under ethics and data-protection approval, with domain specialists and people with lived experience owning the question from the start. Only accountable organisations and professionals can establish the actual governance route and authorise any service or operational decision.
