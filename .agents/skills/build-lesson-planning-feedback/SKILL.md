# Build with the Lesson Planning and Feedback Support playbook

Use this playbook to explore a teaching outcome without turning fictional planning notes into judgements about a teacher, a pupil, or curriculum correctness.

1. Read `content/playbooks/lesson-planning-feedback/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/lesson-planning-feedback/lesson-planning-feedback.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the intended teaching outcome, educational context, and accountable curriculum authority first. Ask when any is not explicit; do not treat urgency, a request for a first build, or a short deadline as a selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional planning notes and state what they cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Preserve teacher authorship and professional judgement. Keep the teacher's purpose, words, edits, decisions, rejection of suggestions, and final responsibility distinguishable from generated or project-authored material.
7. Do not infer pupil ability or need from fictional feedback or lesson-objective records. Do not rank teachers, objectives, subjects, or records as proxies for support need, teaching quality, workload, urgency, or pupil outcomes. Do not treat polished wording as curriculum correctness.
8. Keep feedback provenance explicit. The dataset contains no pupil work or feedback, so do not invent either; name who authored any real source material, suggestion, edit, approval, and final feedback before relying on it.
9. When the builder has not explicitly selected a direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
   - the stated teaching outcome, educational context, and curriculum authority;
   - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
   - known unknowns, including curriculum coverage, teacher workload, feedback provenance, accessibility, pupil privacy, and decision ownership;
   - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or teaching-practice direction when it could meet the outcome;
   - the decisions and outside authority required.
10. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected product or records, ranking, invented fields, schema, routes, screens, interface, workflow, sequence, implementation mechanism, milestones, time-boxed plan, or approval request.
11. If asked what to build first without a selected direction, do not substitute a drafting assistant, planning queue, objective triage, feedback tool, or another application. Return the neutral decision brief.
12. After an explicit choice, test the direction against curriculum authority, subject accuracy, teacher workload, teacher authorship, professional judgement, feedback provenance, accessibility, inclusion, pupil privacy, and safeguarding. Treat unresolved points as unknown.
13. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
14. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real teacher planning, pupil work, feedback about an individual, curriculum-compliance claims, safeguarding decisions, school or data-controller approval, or production use affecting teaching or assessment.

Do not claim to replace teachers, pupils, curriculum or subject specialists, accessibility specialists, safeguarding leads, schools, data controllers, or accountable education decision-makers.
