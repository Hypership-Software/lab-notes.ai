# Build with the Farm Advisory Support playbook

Use this playbook to explore a defined support outcome without turning fictional field-group rows into advice for a farm or field.

1. Read `content/playbooks/farm-advisory/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/farm-advisory/farm-advisory.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the intended outcome, season or decision time, farm and field context, accountable owner, intended users, and accessibility needs first. Ask when any is not explicit; do not treat urgency, a request for which field needs help, or a delivery deadline as a safe selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional working data and state what it cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Distinguish **general guidance** from **farm-specific professional advice**. General guidance may explain public source coverage, evidence gaps, questions, and professional boundaries without saying what a farm should do. Farm-specific advice interprets identified fields, current measurements, season, conditions, farming system, applicable guidance, constraints, and professional judgement to recommend an action.
7. Ask explicitly about seasonality, farm context, and accessibility. The dataset supplies no sample or observation date, crop stage, weather, ground condition, planned action date, holding or field identity, field area or map, soil type, livestock context, input history, costs, farmer observations, connectivity, device, assisted route, language, or non-digital route.
8. Do not identify a farm, field, or fictional field group as needing advice; rank records; invent pH, nitrogen, or yield thresholds; compare unlike yield bands; or recommend a nutrient, product, amount, application rate, timing, location, method, treatment, or other intervention from the synthetic records.
9. If asked which farm or field needs advice or what input or action is best, refuse that part. Do not silently substitute a records checker, review queue, advice-preparation journey, planning tool, calculator, chatbot, or another application.
10. When the builder has not explicitly selected a responsible direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
    - the stated outcome, season or decision time, and farm context;
    - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
    - known unknowns, including field evidence, seasonality, farming context, professional authority, accessibility, representation, and affected stakeholders;
    - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, adviser-mediated, or service-design direction when it could meet the outcome;
    - the decisions and outside authority required.
11. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected product, farm or field selection, ranking, threshold, nutrient or product, rate, amount, timing, location, method, intervention, invented field, schema, route, screen, interface, workflow, model, sequence, milestone, time-boxed plan, approval request, or substitute application.
12. After an explicit choice, test the direction against authoritative guidance, identified field evidence, sample currency and provenance, season and conditions, farming system and local knowledge, environmental and welfare effects, record completeness and representation, accessibility across digital and non-digital routes, and qualified professional authority. Keep fictional records separate from published facts and real farm evidence.
13. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
14. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real holding or field records, maps, soil analysis, input or livestock data, professional interpretation, nutrient or intervention recommendations, animal welfare decisions, environmental or regulatory decisions, legal interpretation, data-controller approval, or action by a farmer, adviser, regulator, or service owner.

Do not claim to replace farmers and their local knowledge, farm workers, qualified advisers or agronomists, animal-welfare or environmental professionals, regulators, legal advisers, data controllers, or accountable service owners.
