# Joined-up Support after a Life Event — domain brief

## Domain vocabulary

- **Death registration:** registering a death at the relevant Registrar's Office, except when the death has been referred to the Coroner. NISRA's quarterly report counts death registrations among Northern Ireland's life-event registrations.
- **District registrar:** the registrar with whom nidirect says a death is registered at the relevant Registrar's Office.
- **Bereavement Service:** the Northern Ireland service that nidirect says records the date of death, informs any benefit office that paid the deceased, and checks whether financial support may be available to the bereaved.
- **Probate:** the process that nidirect says an executor can start applying for when there is a will while sorting out the deceased person's affairs.
- **Executor:** a person usually nominated in the will to sort out the deceased person's affairs.
- **Notification authority:** neither registered source identifies who may authorise each notification or which details may be shared. Treat that authority as unknown until the responsible source owner establishes it.

## Organisations and people affected

nidirect addresses the person dealing with the death or the deceased person's estate. Its guidance names the family doctor, the relevant Registrar's Office, the Bereavement Service, government tax, National Insurance, benefits, rates and vehicle bodies, local councils, banks, building societies, insurers, mortgage and pension providers, landlords, utilities, phone and internet providers, and other organisations connected to the deceased. It also points bereaved people towards practical help from doctors, solicitors, social services and support organisations. Neither registered source describes accessibility needs or measures who needs an accessible or supported route.

## Source map and access

The open nidirect guidance sets out contacts and tasks after a death, including registration, the will, funeral arrangements, the Bereavement Service, government bodies, financial organisations and household providers. The open NISRA quarterly report publishes provisional counts of births, deaths, stillbirths, marriages and civil partnerships registered in each three-month period in Northern Ireland. The NISRA figures count registrations, not end-to-end bereavement journeys. Neither source measures journey completion, delay, consent or drop-off.

## Synthetic fields

`journeyStep` is a synthetic label shaped around steps and contact groups in the nidirect guidance. `medianDays` is a fictional number of days, not an observed or published duration. `dropOffShareBand` is a fictional completion-loss band, not an observed or published proportion. Every timing and every drop-off band in the dataset is fictional.

## Known unknowns

- Who has authority to act or notify at each step is unknown.
- Real completion times and waiting times are not measured by the registered sources.
- Abandonment and incomplete journeys are not measured.
- Duplicate notifications and their consequences are not reported.
- Correction routes after a wrong notification or match are not described.
- Accessibility needs and supported non-digital routes are not measured.
- Disputed estates and changes in who may act are not explained by these sources.

## Assumptions to challenge

- The published checklist does not establish that every bereavement follows one linear journey.
- The sources do not establish that one person has authority throughout.
- Sending a notification does not establish that the receiving organisation has resolved the account or entitlement.
- Registration counts do not establish reliable matching between records held by different organisations.
- Neither source identifies AI as the main missing component.

## Questions before building

- Who authorises each action and which source owner can confirm that authority?
- How is consent or another lawful basis recorded for each disclosure?
- How can a person inspect and correct an error or wrong match?
- Which telephone, face-to-face, postal, assisted and accessible routes must remain available?
- What authority in a published source or from a source owner supports the recipient, field set and proposed intervention?
- Would a clearer checklist, supported hand-off or service integration meet the intended outcome before any AI component is considered?

## Safety and service constraints

Bereavement work must account for distress and avoid premature disclosure of a death. Any joined-up route needs source-owner decisions on authority, data minimisation and permitted recipients before information is shared. Wrongful matching can direct private circumstances or account changes to the wrong person and may create fraud exposure. Accessible and non-digital routes, auditable actions, and a human route to pause, inspect and correct a notification are necessary service constraints; the registered sources do not specify how these controls operate.
