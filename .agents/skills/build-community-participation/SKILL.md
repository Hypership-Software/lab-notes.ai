# Build with the Community Participation Analysis playbook

1. Read `content/playbooks/community-participation/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/community-participation/community-participation.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Ask what outcome the builder wants to explore before proposing an application, topic, data shape, participation route, or implementation.
5. Treat participation volume as different from representativeness. Ask which communities and channels are missing and which non-digital routes exist before interpreting participation.
6. Label each claim as one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify analysis or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional working data and state what it cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** state the assumption and what evidence or authority would be needed.
7. Never infer public opinion, community priorities, prevalence, representativeness, or service performance from the synthetic corpus.
8. When the builder has not explicitly selected an approach, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
   - the stated outcome;
   - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
   - known unknowns, including missing communities, excluded channels, non-digital routes, consent, representation, aggregation, and feedback;
   - at least two parallel credible approaches with trade-offs, kept unranked and including a non-AI or service-design approach when it could meet the outcome;
   - the decisions and outside authority required.
9. End that brief by asking the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Give no recommended or chosen application, selected topic, invented data fields, participation route, workflow, implementation steps, milestone plan, time-boxed build, or approval request before that choice.
10. After an explicit choice, test the selected approach against participation bias, channel exclusion, representation limits, consent, aggregation effects, category contestability, and the route for reporting back. Treat every unresolved item as unknown rather than filling it with plausible detail.
11. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
12. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real submissions, person-level or restricted data, outreach to communities, legal interpretation, a live consultation, publication of findings, or service-owner approval.

Do not claim to replace affected communities, participation practitioners, service owners, policy teams, legal advisers, data controllers, or decision-makers.
