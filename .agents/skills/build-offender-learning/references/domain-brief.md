# Learning Support in Custodial Settings — domain brief

## Domain vocabulary

- **Educational purpose:** the learning goal a direction is intended to support. The strategy example links personalised material with rehabilitation, but the registered sources do not define a curriculum, intervention, or good outcome for a future build.
- **Learner agency:** use this project term for a learner's ability to understand, shape, challenge, change, or stop what happens in their learning. Custody makes the conditions for meaningful choice an explicit question rather than an assumption.
- **Teaching-staff judgement:** the professional decisions teaching staff make about learning goals, provision, support, and progress. A synthetic band or automated suggestion cannot supply this judgement.
- **Custody context:** the practical and institutional setting around learning. The playbook notes that what is running, who is allowed to attend, and whether a sentence is about to end matter but are absent from the dataset.
- **Progress data:** information about an individual learner's participation, work, attainment, support, or development. The committed dataset contains none.
- **Justice or risk decision:** use this project term for a decision about sentence, security, discipline, release, reoffending, or another coercive outcome. Keep it separate from educational support and progress.
- **Personal development plan:** the Learning and skills source says these are used to schedule what each person does. The registered material does not establish what a future tool may infer from or add to one.

## Organisations and people affected

The Department of Justice publishes both registered sources. Its Learning and skills page describes delivery by two further-education colleges under agreement with the Prison Service, essential skills, higher-level distance learning, and personal development plans. Its Prison Service statistics page is the public catalogue for official prison figures and methodology. Neither page publishes learner-level education activity or course-level enrolment and completion counts.

Learners, teaching staff, further-education providers, Prison Service staff, accessibility and safeguarding specialists, data controllers, and accountable education and justice owners could all be affected by a future direction. This is a project stakeholder map, not evidence that any role has agreed to participate. Learners are people with educational goals and rights, not data points for risk, compliance, sentence, or security decisions.

## Source map and access

- **Department of Justice — [Learning and skills](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/topics/learning-and-skills):** open information about learning in Northern Ireland prisons, including delivery by two further-education colleges, essential skills in literacy and numeracy, higher-level distance study, and personal development plans. It publishes no course counts, learner records, completion evidence, or basis for ranking people or selecting an intervention.
- **Department of Justice — [NI Prison Service statistics](https://www.justice-ni.gov.uk/topics/ni-prison-service-statistics):** open official statistics publications and notes about how they are compiled and may be used. The playbook's comparison shows that course-level learning activity is not among the published figures.

Treat both as **Real published source** material. Treat the strategy proposal, playbook summary, purpose, preparation, limitations, and caveats as project interpretation. Treat the committed JSON as **Synthetic working data**, never as a learner record, current provision, capacity evidence, course effectiveness, need, risk, or authority for an educational or justice decision.

## Synthetic fields

- `id` is a fictional record identifier.
- `course` is one of five project-authored course labels drawn from the types of provision named in the playbook. It does not show what is available to any learner or at any establishment.
- `quarter` is one of four fictional 2025 quarters. It is not a published reporting period or evidence of when a course ran.
- `enrolmentsBand` is an invented course-level range. No registered source publishes a corresponding value.
- `completionBand` is an invented course-level percentage range. No registered source publishes a corresponding value, and the dataset gives no definition of completion.

The 20 rows contain no learner, establishment, class, teacher, goal, curriculum, accessibility need, attendance, work, assessment, progress, sentence, security condition, release information, intervention, or outcome. Do not create fictional learner scenarios to bridge that gap. Do not infer provision, capacity, demand, quality, effectiveness, ability, need, engagement, reoffending, risk, eligibility, priority, or a next course from the bands.

## Known unknowns

- The educational purpose, custody context, learner group, accountable owner, and permitted decision are unknown until stated.
- Current course availability, capacity, schedule, location, access conditions, and continuity are not published in the registered sources.
- Learner goals, prior learning, language, literacy, numeracy, accessibility, digital access, support, work, participation, and progress are absent.
- The curriculum, pedagogy, learning resources, teaching capacity, assessment approach, and evidence for any intervention are not established.
- How learners exercise meaningful choice, see and correct information, challenge suggestions, decline participation, or seek another route is not defined.
- How teaching staff retain authorship and professional judgement, and what information they genuinely need, remains unknown.
- No purpose, lawful basis, minimisation rule, access model, retention period, correction route, data-controller decision, or protection against secondary use exists for real progress data.
- The boundaries between education and sentence, security, discipline, release, rehabilitation, or reoffending decisions are not established by the sources.
- The registered sources do not establish that a learning dashboard, review queue, capacity view, planning tool, software product, or AI is required.

## Assumptions to challenge

- A low fictional completion band does not identify a struggling learner, weak course, urgent need, or intervention priority.
- An enrolment band does not establish demand, capacity, access, popularity, or educational value.
- Course-level aggregates cannot predict an individual's progress, engagement, needs, choices, or likelihood of reoffending.
- Learning participation or progress is not evidence of compliance, behaviour, rehabilitation, sentence readiness, security risk, or release suitability.
- A transparent score, staff override, or learner-facing explanation cannot make an unsupported risk feature educationally valid.
- Pseudonyms and fictional scenarios do not make sensitive combined learning, custody, and justice histories appropriate starter data.
- Staff involvement does not replace learner agency, and learner assent in custody cannot be assumed from the availability of an interface action.
- Manual teaching practice, participatory service inquiry, clearer access information, or deciding not to build may meet the outcome without learner profiling or AI.

## Questions before building

- What educational purpose is intended, in which custody context, for which learners, and who owns the decision?
- Which learning goals matter to learners, and how will learners shape, challenge, change, or stop a direction?
- What curriculum, pedagogy, provision, teaching capacity, and professional evidence support the intended outcome?
- What practical custody constraints affect access or continuity, and who has authority to describe them without turning them into learner risk features?
- What will teaching staff decide, and what information supports rather than displaces their judgement?
- Could staff-led learning review, learner co-design, service mapping, clearer provision information, or another non-tool direction meet the outcome?
- What learner or progress data is genuinely necessary, and who can establish its purpose, access, correction, retention, deletion, and protection from secondary justice use?
- How will educational data remain separate from sentence, security, discipline, release, rehabilitation, reoffending, and other coercive decisions?
- What accessible, language-supported, digital, paper, and assisted routes would learners need?
- Where must repository work stop because learner participation, teaching judgement, real provision, custody authority, safeguarding, or data-controller decisions are unavailable?

## Safety and service constraints

Keep all 20 rows visibly labelled **Synthetic working data** and separate from the two real published sources. Do not invent learner records, custody histories, progress events, scores, rankings, thresholds, interventions, deadlines, or target lists. Do not use aggregate course bands to select a person, course, resource, support offer, or operational action.

Keep educational purpose, participation, and progress separate from sentence, security, discipline, release, rehabilitation, reoffending, and risk decisions. Make learner agency and teaching-staff judgement explicit before implementation choices. Minimise and protect any future real progress data, prevent secondary coercive use, and stop until accountable education providers, the Prison Service, data controllers, and affected learners establish the necessary purpose and authority. Do not prescribe a dashboard, queue, capacity view, planning tool, or other substitute when the responsible direction has not been chosen.
