# Build with the Adaptive Tutoring playbook

Use this playbook to explore a learning outcome without turning fictional topic summaries into judgements about a pupil or a prescribed intervention.

1. Read `content/playbooks/adaptive-tutoring/playbook.ts`.
2. Read `content/playbooks/adaptive-tutoring/adaptive-tutoring.data.json`.
3. Read `references/domain-brief.md` completely.
4. Establish the intended learning outcome and educational context first. Ask when either is not explicit; do not treat urgency, a request for a first build, or a short deadline as a selected direction.
5. Separate every claim into one of these evidence classes:
   - **Published:** attribute it to a registered source in the playbook.
   - **Project interpretation:** identify content or a constraint authored by this repository.
   - **Synthetic:** identify fictional aggregate topic data and state what it cannot establish.
   - **Inferred or unsupported:** name the assumption and the evidence or authority needed.
6. Separate learning support from assessment or pupil profiling. Do not use the fictional topic, difficulty, attempt, or success bands to identify pupils, infer ability or need, rank topics, choose a learner's next activity, or prescribe an intervention.
7. Ask how teachers, learners, and guardians retain meaningful control. Keep teacher judgement, learner voice, accessible alternatives, challenge and correction routes, and age-appropriate guardian involvement open until the responsible people define them.
8. When the builder has not explicitly selected a direction, return only a neutral decision brief in this order:
   - the stated learning outcome and educational context;
   - published evidence, project interpretation, and synthetic-data boundaries;
   - known unknowns, including pedagogy, curriculum progression, accessibility, pupil privacy, and decision ownership;
   - at least two parallel, unranked outcome directions. Give each only three parts: intended outcome, published evidence or outside authority needed, and trade-offs. Include a manual, non-tool, or teaching-practice direction when it could meet the outcome;
   - the decisions and outside authority required.
9. Ask the builder to choose a direction, then stop. Before that choice, give no recommendation, selected topic or product, invented pupil events or fields, cue rules, schema, routes, screens, interface, workflow, sequence, implementation mechanism, milestones, time-boxed plan, or approval request.
10. If asked what to build first without a selected direction, do not substitute a fractions pathway, intervention cue, next-activity tool, tutor, or another application. Return the neutral decision brief.
11. After an explicit choice, test the direction against pedagogical purpose, curriculum progression, accessibility, pupil privacy, teacher judgement, learner agency, guardian involvement, and the difference between support and assessment. Treat unresolved points as unknown.
12. Follow `AGENTS.md`, the shared typed playbook contract, privacy rules, accessibility requirements, and repository quality gates before changing code.
13. Stop and name the missing authority when work requires real pupil records, assessment or profiling, safeguarding decisions, curriculum claims, school or data-controller approval, or production use affecting what a learner is taught.

Do not claim to replace teachers, learners, guardians, curriculum specialists, accessibility specialists, safeguarding leads, schools, data controllers, or accountable education decision-makers.
